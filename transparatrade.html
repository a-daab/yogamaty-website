1. Executive Summary

Modern slavery and the climate crisis are not separate problems — they are two expressions of the same root cause: economic systems that permit the exploitation of the powerless for the profit of the powerful. TransparaTrade™ proposes a unified, systemic response: mandatory supply chain transparency labeling on all consumer products, enforced through blockchain-verified data collection and a global multi-stakeholder governance framework centered on survivor expertise.

"TransparaTrade™ is more than a system — it is a Global Table of Peace, where survivors, governments, corporations, academics, lawyers, technologists, artists, and citizens come together as equals within a circular leadership model that centers survivor lived experience."

The immediate action we advocate for is simple: require that every product sold carry a verifiable label showing who made it, how much they were paid, how many hours they worked, and what environmental impact was created at each step. This single change, if legislated globally, would make exploitation structurally unprofitable.

2. The Dual Crisis: People & Planet

Approximately 50 million people live in conditions of modern slavery today. The majority are embedded in global supply chains — working in garment factories, mining rare earth metals, harvesting agricultural products, or producing consumer goods that ultimately reach the shelves of wealthy nations. These workers are largely invisible to consumers, by design.

The People Crisis

50M+ people in modern slavery. Most in supply chains of products sold in wealthy nations. Rendered invisible by design.

The Planet Crisis

Global supply chains account for ~80% of global trade emissions. Environmental data is similarly obscured from consumers.

These two crises share a common mechanism: the absence of accountability. When consumers cannot see the conditions under which their purchases were produced, there is no market signal against exploitation. Conversely, when supply chains are fully transparent, ethical sourcing becomes a competitive advantage, and exploitation becomes a liability.

The climate crisis compounds this further: the regions where labor exploitation is most severe are disproportionately exposed to climate risk. Addressing one without the other is not only incomplete — it is futile.

3. What is TransparaTrade™?

TransparaTrade™ is a framework with three operational components:

  • A Transparency Standard: A defined set of data points that must be collected and disclosed at each step of a product's supply chain — including worker identity, wages, hours, safety conditions, and environmental metrics (CO₂, water, waste, land use, chemical discharge).
  • A Technology Infrastructure: A blockchain-based data collection and verification system that records supply chain data in real time as products move through the supply chain, making it immutable, auditable, and publicly accessible.
  • A Governance Model: The Global Table of Peace — a circular multi-stakeholder body in which survivors hold permanent co-leadership alongside governments, corporations, legal experts, technologists, civil society, and citizens.

TransparaTrade™ does not require trust in corporations. It requires verification. The blockchain infrastructure means that data cannot be falsified after the fact — each entry is timestamped, geolocated, and signed by the recording party. Independent auditors and third-party certification bodies provide additional verification layers.

4. The Global Table of Peace

At the center of TransparaTrade™ is a governance structure unlike any that has governed global supply chains before: one in which survivors of modern slavery are not consulted as advisors, but seated as co-equals at every decision-making table.

The circular leadership model operates without a fixed hierarchy. Survivors, governments, corporations, academics, lawyers, technologists, artists, and citizens each hold a defined role. Decisions require consensus across constituencies. No single group can override another.

"United by transparency, accountability, and collaboration, this model ensures that no one is unseen and no contribution is too small."

The Table operates at three levels: global (policy and standards), regional (implementation and enforcement), and local (community liaison and worker verification). Each level reports to the one above and feeds evidence upward — from the worker on a loom in Bhaktapur to the treaty negotiator in Geneva.

5. The Blockchain Solution

Traditional supply chain auditing is periodic, expensive, and easily gamed. A factory may pass an audit conducted quarterly while violating standards every other week. TransparaTrade™ replaces periodic auditing with continuous, real-time data recording using distributed ledger technology.

Each node in the supply chain — from raw material supplier to packaging facility — is equipped with a verified data entry interface. Worker check-ins, wage payments, environmental readings, and quality control events are logged to the chain as they happen. The data is accessible to consumers through a simple QR code scan on the product label.

The TransparaTrade™ allies team is currently building this infrastructure in open-source, with the goal of making it available to any brand at zero cost. We seek partners across software engineering, blockchain architecture, IoT hardware, UX design, legal compliance, and sustainability measurement.

6. The YogaMaty Pilot

The YogaMaty is the first real-world implementation of the TransparaTrade™ framework. It is a hand-woven yoga mat produced in Nepal, sold internationally, and accompanied by a QR code that opens a full, interactive supply chain record for the buyer's specific production batch.

The pilot demonstrates three things:

  1. It is technically feasible. Real supply chain data can be collected, verified, and presented to consumers in a format that is engaging, useful, and trusted.
  2. It is commercially viable. Consumers respond positively to transparency. Brands that embrace it gain loyalty; brands that resist it face reputational pressure.
  3. It is scalable. The infrastructure built for the YogaMaty can be extended to any product category, any supply chain complexity, and any jurisdiction.

7. Policy Advocacy & Goals

TransparaTrade™'s core policy goal is mandatory supply chain transparency labeling legislation in every jurisdiction that participates in global trade. This means:

  • Every product sold must carry a verifiable transparency label (physical QR code or digital equivalent) disclosing supply chain data meeting the TransparaTrade™ standard.
  • Brands are legally liable for the accuracy of disclosed data. False disclosure is treated as consumer fraud.
  • A global registry of certified supply chain transparency providers — including blockchain verification services — is maintained and publicly accessible.
  • Governments that adopt the standard receive preferential trade terms with partner nations. Non-adoption increases trade friction over time.

We are currently engaged in advocacy conversations with policymakers in the European Union (which already has the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive), the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, and Australia. We welcome introductions to any policymaker or legislative counsel working in this space.

8. How to Join

TransparaTrade™ is open to all. The allies team is a global, distributed group of individuals and organizations committed to building the technology, legal frameworks, and policy environment that will make mandatory supply chain transparency a reality.

We are actively recruiting allies with expertise in:

  • Blockchain architecture and distributed ledger technology
  • Supply chain management and logistics
  • Labor rights law and international trade law
  • Sustainability measurement and environmental science
  • UX/UI design and consumer technology
  • Policy advocacy and government relations
  • Survivor advocacy and trauma-informed practice
  • Brand and communications

There is no fee to join. There is no minimum commitment. There is only the willingness to contribute whatever you can, for as long as you can, toward a world where every product on every shelf tells the whole truth.
